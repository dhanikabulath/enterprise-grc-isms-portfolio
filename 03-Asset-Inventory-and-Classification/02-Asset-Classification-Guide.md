# Asset Classification Guide

## Purpose

This guide explains how Stark Industries classifies information and technology assets within the ISMS.

Asset classification helps determine how strongly an asset needs to be protected based on its sensitivity, business use, and security requirements.

## Classification Levels

### Confidential

Confidential assets are limited to authorised personnel.

Examples within Stark Industries include:

- source code;
- application data;
- AWS storage containing sensitive information; and
- other information where unauthorised disclosure could affect the company.

Access should be limited to people who need the information for their work.

### Restricted

Restricted assets are used for operational purposes but require controlled access.

Examples include:

- the SaaS platform;
- AWS infrastructure;
- CI/CD systems;
- GitHub repositories and supporting development services;
- security logs.

Access should be based on job responsibilities and business need.

### Internal Use Only

Internal Use Only assets are intended for general employee use but should not be publicly available.

Examples include:

- employee laptops;
- internal tools;
- routine internal working information.

Employees are expected to handle these assets according to company policies.

### Public

Public information has been approved for external release.

This may include information intended for customers or the public where disclosure does not create a security concern.

## Asset Ownership

Each asset should have an identified owner.

The asset owner is responsible for:

- making sure the asset is registered;
- assigning the appropriate classification;
- making sure suitable protection is applied;
- reporting risks or significant changes affecting the asset.

## Asset Handling

Users must follow the company's Acceptable Use Policy when handling information and technology assets.

Sensitive information should be protected according to its classification.

Where appropriate:

- access should be limited;
- information should be encrypted;
- storage media should be protected;
- lost or compromised assets should be reported; and
- information should be securely disposed of when no longer required.

## Asset Lifecycle

Assets should be tracked from acquisition through normal use to decommissioning.

When an asset is no longer required, it should be securely removed or disposed of and the relevant records updated.

## Review

The asset inventory and classification should be reviewed at least annually and when significant changes occur to the systems or business environment.
